Ninja Character -Full Production Walkthrough

A complete breakdown of how a character goes from reference board to engine-ready wardrobe asset, step by step.

1
PureRef
Reference Collection
Before touching any 3D software, I build a dense reference board in PureRef -historical ninja garments, fabric types, silhouettes, movement references, and real-world kenjutsu attire. Solid references prevent guess-work downstream.
2
ZBrush
Character Sculpting
High-poly sculpting of the character's head, body, and face. Anatomy knowledge is applied here -musculature under clothing, proportional balance, and realistic surface detail that will transfer through baking.
3
Marvelous Designer
Clothing Creation
Garments are constructed from real 2D pattern pieces, sewn together in MD, and simulated on the character body. This produces physically accurate cloth behaviour -drape, tension, fold lines -that would be impossible to model by hand.
4
ZBrush
Clothing Detailing
The simulated cloth mesh is brought into ZBrush to add surface detail -fabric weave, stitching, folds, wrinkles, and material texture that Marvelous Designer doesn't produce. This high-frequency detail bakes down onto the low-poly later.
5
Blender
Retopology
The high-density simulation mesh is retopologised in Blender to a clean, game-ready low-poly mesh. Edge loops follow the garment seams and animation deformation areas to preserve silhouette and fold shape at runtime.
6
Marmoset Toolbag
Baking (High → Low)
Normal maps, AO, curvature, and thickness baked from the high-poly onto the game-ready low-poly in Marmoset. Cage-based baking handles tight geometry and layered garment areas that standard bakers struggle with.
7
Substance Painter
Texturing
PBR texture set creation -fabric weave patterns, wear and soiling, stitching detail, and material variation across linen, leather, and metal trim. Smart materials layered with hand-painted breakup.
8
Unreal Engine + MH Creator
Character Export & Skin Wrapping
Character exported to UE, then skin-wrapped using MH Creator to bind the clothing mesh correctly to the character skeleton. This ensures deformation follows anatomy rather than just bone transforms.
9
Blender
Clothing Skinning
Character exported back to Blender for precise clothing skinning -weight painting each garment piece to the skeleton with attention to layered cloth, secondary motion, and joint deformation at elbows, knees, and hips.
10
Unreal Engine + MH Wardrobe
Final Engine Integration
Final clothing export to UE and assembled as an MH Wardrobe entry -LOD setup, material assignments, and in-engine validation to confirm performance and visual quality at all LOD levels.
